Until 21 October there was a fallback in the matching: where a SKU matched nothing, Fusion tried to create an item under the Shopify product's name, QuickBooks answered “duplicate name”, and it then used whatever carried that name — including an item already holding a different SKU. So a Shopify line with SP-21 could land on the QuickBooks item whose own SKU is SP-21-50: a different product, at a different price, with nothing said anywhere.

That fallback is gone — matching is the SKU and nothing else, and nothing is ever created. But the invoices it wrote are in your books. This reads them back out of QuickBooks and shows every line that disagrees with what its SKU says today. What to do about one is yours to decide, in QuickBooks.

It leaves no trace in QuickBooks. Nothing is written, nothing is changed, and no record of the check is kept there — it only reads. And it only reads when you press the button: it is not on a timer and it does not run when this page opens, because reading your whole invoice list is not something to do every half minute. The invoices are fetched by date range, so a few hundred of them are a few calls rather than a few hundred.

Where each side comes from. The invoice — what is actually on the line — is read live out of QuickBooks, not from anything kept here. What it should have been comes from two places: the order’s Shopify lines in Fusion’s own table, and your QuickBooks product list as it is today. So an order whose Shopify lines have since been cleared out cannot be checked, and is counted separately below rather than passed.
With this on, an order placed on Shopify becomes an invoice in QuickBooks by itself, within seconds. The page where you pick dates stays for filling in a month that was missed.

Which orders

Nothing placed before this day is ever written, so turning the writing on does not go back over every order already here. What to leave out — voided, refunded, not shipped yet — is ticked on the orders page, beside the days, and the same ticks apply here. Cancelled orders are never written at all.

What the invoice says

Who the invoices are made out to and where delivery lands are set on the orders page, beside the button that makes them — one place, and the automatic writing uses the same answers. The invoice number is Shopify’s own, character for character, so there is nothing to set for it.

A product that is not in the books yet

Products are matched on the SKU and nothing else — the same SKU is used on both sides, so the two may call a thing by different names and the SKU says they are the same thing.

Nothing is ever made or changed in QuickBooks on its own. No item is created by an order, by the sweep or by a webhook, and no SKU is ever written onto an item that already exists. A SKU that matches nothing stops that one order, names the SKU under Needs you, and goes through by itself the moment that SKU is on the right item in QuickBooks.

You can add a product, and only you. A product saved in Shopify appears under New products within seconds, showing exactly what will be typed into the books — name, one description for both the sales and the purchasing side, type, price, cost — and nothing is created until you press Sync now on that row. The accounts below are the ones it will use.
